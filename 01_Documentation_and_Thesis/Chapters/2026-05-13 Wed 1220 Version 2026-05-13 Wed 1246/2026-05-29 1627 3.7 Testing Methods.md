## 3.7 Testing Methods

To systematically evaluate the performance, reliability, and resiliency of the Modular Adaptive Data Node (MADN) prototype under simulated and real-world conditions representative of Bulawayo and Tsholotsho, a series of rigorous testing methods were established. These testing protocols target the sensor data accuracy, inference latency, alert transmission speeds, database write transaction integrity, offline wireless communication limits, and power consumption profiles.

---

### 3.7.1 Telemetry Verification and Inference Latency Testing

The validation of the Agri-Analytics module is divided into hardware sensor testing and local machine learning model evaluation:

1.  **Sensor Accuracy Calibration**:
    *   *Capacitive Soil Moisture Calibration*: Probes were placed in three soil samples with differing hydration levels (bone dry, optimal dampness, and fully saturated). Sensor analog voltage readouts were recorded via the Pico W ADC0 channel and compared to gravimetric soil water measurements (drying soil in an oven and weighing the water loss) to map raw ADC values to percentage volumetric water content (% VWC).
    *   *DHT22 Accuracy Verification*: Digital readings of relative humidity and temperature were recorded concurrently with measurements from a calibrated, industrial-grade thermohygrometer. Readings were taken over a 24-hour cycle to capture high-temperature midday and low-temperature night fluctuations, calculating mean absolute error (MAE) and root mean square error (RMSE).
2.  **TensorFlow Lite Inference Benchmarking**:
    *   *Inference Latency Measurement*: A test script on the central Raspberry Pi 4 recorded the execution time of the TF Lite interpreter interpreter-only library when loading the quantized `watering_model.tflite` model and executing classifications. Latency was measured over 1,000 consecutive iterations to calculate average inference execution time, standard deviation, and maximum jitter.
    *   *Prediction Accuracy Evaluation*: The binary watering decisions output by the model (water vs. do not water) were compared to a manual decision matrix validated by agricultural extension charts to evaluate model precision, recall, and F1-score under varying input parameters.

---

### 3.7.2 Alert Propagation and Security Trigger Delay

To verify that the Edge-Alert Security Gateway functions as a low-latency trigger system, alert propagation times were measured across the physical and network boundaries:

1.  **Trigger Latency Measurement**:
    *   An automated timing script on the edge Pico W flagged the exact microsecond timestamp at which GP14 (PIR digital input) or GP16/17 (Ultrasonic distance change exceeding threshold) was triggered.
    *   This timestamp was compiled into the JSON payload sent via MQTT.
    *   The central Vault daemon script recorded the arrival timestamp upon receiving the MQTT trigger.
    *   The difference between the trigger timestamp and the arrival timestamp was calculated as the net network propagation latency. This test was repeated 500 times to determine average network delay under nominal Wi-Fi signal conditions.
2.  **Virtual Tripwire Detection Range**:
    *   The HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor was mounted at a height of 1 meter. Physical obstacles of varying profiles (e.g., human-sized targets, small animals) were introduced at distances ranging from 50cm to 400cm at angles of 0°, 15°, and 30° relative to the sensor axis to map the detection boundary and record the false-negative rate.

---

### 3.7.3 Transactional Resiliency and Rollback Validation

To verify the transaction integrity of the Mesh-POS database system in environments with high power instability, we tested checkout transaction completion and SQLite ACID compliance under simulated power disruptions:

1.  **POS Communication Latency**:
    *   Using a test client on a guest mobile device, 100 checkouts were simulated over the local Wi-Fi Access Point (via WebSockets) and Bluetooth SPP (via RFCOMM socket).
    *   The total round-trip time—from entering the transaction total to receiving a signed SQLite invoice confirmation on the client screen—was measured to calculate transaction execution latency.
2.  **ACID Transaction Resiliency under Power Loss**:
    *   To simulate sudden grid failure or power bank disconnection, a hardware switch was wired to interrupt the USB-C power delivery rails of the central Raspberry Pi 4 orchestrator.
    *   A continuous script on the mobile client sent rapid checkout transaction batches.
    *   While the SQLite database transaction writes were actively executing (`COMMIT` command execution), power to the Pi 4 was cut.
    *   Upon rebooting the Pi 4, the database journal and transaction ledger were inspected to verify that no half-written or corrupt transaction states existed, proving that SQLite's Write-Ahead Logging (WAL) and automatic transaction rollback rolled back incomplete records safely.

---

### 3.7.4 Offline Mesh Network Throughput and Signal Range

The offline local network was tested to establish operational limits and connection stability in rural setups:

1.  **Packet Delivery Rate vs. Distance**:
    *   An edge Pico W node was placed at increasing distances (from 10 meters to 150 meters) in direct line-of-sight and obstructed (behind brick walls and foliage) environments relative to the central Pi 4 Access Point.
    *   The node published 1,000 MQTT packets at 1-second intervals.
    *   The packet loss rate and Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI) values were logged on the central broker to chart network degradation.
2.  **Reconnection Recovery Latency**:
    *   A Pico W node was powered down, moved out of Wi-Fi range, and powered back up.
    *   The node was physically moved back into range.
    *   The time from re-entering the AP signal threshold to establishing a socket handshake with the Mosquitto broker and resuming MQTT telemetry transmission was recorded to define connection recovery latency.

---

### 3.7.5 Power Profiling and Battery Lifespan Projections

Because the edge nodes are deployed in remote fields powered by swappable batteries, accurate current profiling is required to maximize operational life:

1.  **Current Draw Profiling**:
    *   A digital multimeter was connected in series between the TP4056 step-up output rail and the Pico W VBUS pin.
    *   Current draw was recorded during three operational states:
        *   *Deep Sleep State*: MicroPython `machine.deepsleep()` active, sensors powered down (measured in microamperes).
        *   *Active Sampling State*: Sensors active, reading ADC and digital pins (measured in milliamperes).
        *   *RF Transmission State*: Local Wi-Fi transceiver active, connecting to the AP and publishing MQTT packets (measured in milliamperes).
2.  **Battery Discharge Cycle Verification**:
    *   Fully charged 18650 Li-Ion cells (4.2V fully charged, 2500mAh nominal) were connected to edge nodes.
    *   The nodes were programmed to sample sensors every 15 minutes, publish to the broker, and enter deep sleep.
    *   The battery voltage was reported as a telemetry variable and recorded in InfluxDB.
    *   The battery was discharged until the TP4056 protection module cutoff threshold (typically 2.7V) was reached, logging the net runtime (in days) to validate theoretical battery lifespan calculations.
