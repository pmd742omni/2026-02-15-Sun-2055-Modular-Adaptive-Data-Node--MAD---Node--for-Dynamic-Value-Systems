1.2 Problem Statement

​Despite the high penetration of smartphones, laptops, and other personal computing devices in Bulawayo and Tsholotsho, a vast reservoir of local computational power remains idle and unutilized for the majority of the day. Concurrently, these regions are hindered by critical, data-intensive challenges in three primary sectors: agriculture suffers from a lack of localized, microclimate-specific data processing to determine watering requirements and manage soil hydration; public security lacks intelligent, real-time edge processing for perimeter surveillance; and commercial vendors struggle with transaction inefficiencies and inventory management due to unreliable connectivity and the absence of resilient point-of-sale (POS) systems.
​The core problem is that existing technological interventions rely heavily on expensive, centralized cloud computing architectures. These traditional systems are ill-suited for the local socio-economic and infrastructure realities, often failing due to intermittent internet access and high data costs. Currently, there is no viable, localized framework designed to seamlessly aggregate and orchestrate the latent processing capacity of ubiquitous personal devices to solve these regional issues.
​Without a modular, adaptive system capable of horizontally scaling and utilizing this decentralized power, communities in Bulawayo and Tsholotsho remain dependent on fragile external infrastructure. Consequently, local farmers, security personnel, and small-scale vendors continue to operate sub-optimally, unable to leverage the powerful computing resources physically present within their own environments. This project addresses the critical absence of a decentralized, edge-orchestrated network specifically, the Modular Adaptive Data Node (MADN) needed to bridge the gap between idle local hardware and the pressing computational demands of regional agriculture, security, and commerce.
