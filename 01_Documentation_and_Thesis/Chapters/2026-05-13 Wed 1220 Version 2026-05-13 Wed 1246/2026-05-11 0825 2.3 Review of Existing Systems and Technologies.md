2.3 Review of Existing Systems and Technologies
Current technological interventions designed to solve localized challenges predominantly utilize
centralized models:
• Cloud-Reliant Precision Agriculture: Traditional agricultural optimization platforms
process generalized regional data on remote servers [22]. While these models are technically
robust, they require constant internet connectivity to transmit raw environmental metrics
and receive actionable planting or irrigation insights back from the cloud [4].
• Centralized Public Security Surveillance: Existing security and perimeter defense tech
nologies heavily rely on traditional CCTV systems [23]. These architectures transmit con
tinuous, high-bandwidth video feeds to central servers for storage and processing, relying on
constant internet uplinks to function effectively [6, 23].
• Cloud-Dependent Point-of-Sale (POS) Systems: Modern commercial trade and digital
inventory tools are engineered to synchronize with remote databases. Informal and small-scale
vendors currently utilize POS systems that demand stable internet connections to validate
transactions and update stock [7, 12].
Taken together, these systems demonstrate that contemporary digital interventions are often tech
nically sophisticated yet operationally dependent on assumptions of stable connectivity, persistent
cloud access, and adequate financial capacity for data-intensive services [4, 7, 22, 23]. This creates
a tension between what such systems can achieve under ideal conditions and what communities
can sustainably use under infrastructural constraint. The literature therefore suggests that the core
limitation is not the absence of advanced digital tools themselves, but the persistence of architec
tural models whose success depends on infrastructural conditions that cannot be assumed in the
target setting [14, 30, 33, 35].
