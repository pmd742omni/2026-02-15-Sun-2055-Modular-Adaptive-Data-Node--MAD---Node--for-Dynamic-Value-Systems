​1.6 Scope of the Project

​The scope of this project is defined by geographical, hardware, functional, and target demographic boundaries to ensure a focused and achievable implementation:

​Geographical Boundary: The deployment, testing, and data modelling for the system will be strictly localized to the specific microclimates, urban environments, and peri-urban agricultural zones of Bulawayo and the Tsholotsho district.

​Hardware Boundary: The physical infrastructure is constrained to a core "seed" setup comprising one (1) Raspberry Pi 4 Model B acting as the central orchestration hub, and two (2) Raspberry Pi Pico W microcontrollers acting as edge "leaf" nodes. The scope also includes the software-level integration of existing third-party idle devices (e.g., local smartphones and laptops) acting as temporary computational "guest" nodes and mobile POS client terminals. The project will not involve the design or manufacturing of custom printed circuit boards (PCBs).

​Functional Boundary: The software and system architecture will focus exclusively on developing the four proposed pillars:
​The MADN Orchestrator (Dynamic Resource Scheduler, API Gateway).
​The Agri-Analytics Engine (MQTT telemetry, InfluxDB/Grafana, and TensorFlow Lite irrigation and watering requirement modeling).
The Edge-Alert Security Gateway (PIR/ultrasonic sensor integration and real-time state alerts).
​The Mesh-POS System (Distributed Micro-Transaction Ledger and Inventory Synchronization).

​Exclusions: The project deliberately excludes cloud-dependent processing paradigms. All computation must be edge-native or localized. Furthermore, while the MADN is a modular framework, this initial study will not develop applications outside of the three targeted sectors (agriculture, security, commerce); other potential domains such as healthcare or smart transportation are beyond the current scope.
