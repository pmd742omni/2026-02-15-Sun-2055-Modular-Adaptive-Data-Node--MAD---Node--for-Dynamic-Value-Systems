2.7 Summary of Literature Review
The literature demonstrates that while complex data-driven solutions exist for agriculture, security, and commerce, their reliance on centralized cloud infrastructures renders them largely ineffective for regions experiencing intermittent connectivity and high bandwidth costs [4, 30]. At the same time, the reviewed studies show that edge intelligence, distributed computing, embedded machine learning, and cloud elasticity are not competing paradigms, but complementary components of a more adaptive computational architecture [26, 31, 33, 37]. Without a modular system capable of coordinating these components while utilizing decentralized local power, communities in Bulawayo and Tsholotsho remain dependent on fragile external infrastructure [16]. This underscores the critical necessity for a localized yet hybrid edge–cloud network specifically, the Modular Adaptive Data Node (MADN) to bridge the gap between idle local hardware, constrained infrastructure, and the pressing computational demands of the region [17, 32, 33]. In this sense, the chapter establishes not only the relevance of MADN, but also the design logic that should guide the chapters that follow: computation must be local, when necessary, collaborative where possible, and cloud augmented when advantageous [16, 31, 33, 37].
