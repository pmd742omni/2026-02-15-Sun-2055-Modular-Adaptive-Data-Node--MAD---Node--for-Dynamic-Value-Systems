2.5 Gaps in Existing Solutions

A critical review of the existing technological landscape reveals significant shortcomings when
applied to resource-constrained environments:
• Vulnerability to Infrastructure Realities: Existing technological interventions rely heav
ily on expensive, centralized cloud computing architectures [13]. These traditional systems
are ill-suited for local socio-economic and infrastructure realities, often failing due to inter
mittent internet access and high data costs [14]. Cloud-dependent paradigms often falter in
regions like Bulawayo and Tsholotsho due to latency issues and connectivity blackouts [4, 30].
• Wasted Local Compute Resources: Despite the high penetration of smartphones, lap
tops, and other personal computing devices in Bulawayo and Tsholotsho, a vast reservoir of
local computational power remains idle and unutilized for most of the day [10]. Currently,
there is no viable, localized framework designed to seamlessly aggregate and orchestrate the
latent processing capacity of ubiquitous personal devices to solve regional issues [15].
• Lack of Microclimate Specificity: In agriculture, existing solutions suffer from a lack of
localized, microclimate-specific data processing for soil hydration and irrigation optimization [11]. Farmers require
precise, microclimate-specific insights to manage soil moisture levels and determine watering needs, rather than generalized regional
data [5, 22].
• Absence of Offline Operational Resilience: Public security lacks intelligent, real-time
edge processing for perimeter surveillance that does not rely on constant internet uplinks [6,
12]. Furthermore, commercial vendors struggle with transaction inefficiencies and inventory
management due to unreliable connectivity and the absence of resilient POS systems that
maintain transaction integrity during blackouts [7, 12].
