2.6 Implications for MADN Design
The reviewed literature does more than identify a technological gap; it clarifies the architectural
principles that MADN must embody. First, the framework must preserve local operational con
tinuity, ensuring that essential sensing, inference, coordination, and transaction functions remain
available even when internet access is unstable or absent [6, 12, 35]. Second, it must support
hybrid edge–cloud adaptability, allowing tasks to be retained locally when latency, privacy, or con
nectivity constraints dominate, while still offloading appropriate workloads to cloud infrastructure
when elasticity, storage, or large-scale coordination become advantageous [31, 33, 36, 37]. Third, it
must enable distributed utilization of latent resources, so that idle consumer devices can contribute
meaningful computational value within a localized network rather than leaving communities wholly
dependent on external infrastructure [3, 15, 32].
In design terms, these implications suggest that MADN should not be framed merely as a software
platform, but as a resilient orchestration layer across heterogeneous devices, services, and connectiv
ity states. Its novelty lies in combining edge intelligence, decentralized participation, and selective
cloud augmentation into a system that is responsive to local conditions while still capable of scaling
outward when infrastructure permits [16, 17, 33, 37]. This interpretation positions MADN as a
practical response to the central limitation identified across the literature: the mismatch between
advanced digital systems and the environments in which they are expected to operate. It further
implies that orchestration is not a secondary implementation concern, but a core design princi
ple through which MADN can decide what should execute locally, what should be shared across
nearby devices, and what should be escalated to cloud infrastructure under favorable conditions
[16, 31, 33, 37]
