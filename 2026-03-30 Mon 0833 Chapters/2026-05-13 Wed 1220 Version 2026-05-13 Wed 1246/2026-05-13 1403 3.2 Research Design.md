## 3.2 Research Design
The research design for the Modular Adaptive Data Node (MADN) is rooted in the **Design Science Research (DSR)** paradigm. This approach is particularly suited for information systems and engineering projects where the primary objective is to create and evaluate an innovative artifact—in this case, a decentralized edge computing framework—to solve a practical, localized problem. By utilizing DSR, the study ensures a rigorous balance between creative engineering and scientific validation.

The development process follows a systematic iterative cycle, consisting of several distinct phases:

1.  **Problem Identification and Motivation:** This phase involved analyzing the computational resource gap in Bulawayo and Tsholotsho, where idle personal device power remains untapped while critical sectors like agriculture and security lack data-driven solutions.
2.  **Definition of the Objectives for a Solution:** Specific technical goals were established, including the creation of a tiered orchestrator-edge node architecture, an agri-analytics engine, and a distributed micro-transaction ledger.
3.  **Design and Development:** This is the core technical phase where the hardware (Raspberry Pi 4 and Pico W) is integrated and the software modules (Dynamic Resource Scheduler, Mesh-POS, etc.) are programmed. A modular design philosophy is used to ensure system flexibility and scalability.
4.  **Demonstration:** The MADN prototype is deployed in simulated and controlled real-world scenarios to demonstrate its ability to solve the identified problems (e.g., real-time moisture monitoring or offline transaction processing).
5.  **Evaluation:** The system's performance is rigorously measured against established benchmarks, focusing on processing latency, power efficiency, mesh network stability, and data accuracy.

This research design employs a **mixed-methods approach** for data collection. Quantitative data is gathered through automated system logs, performance metrics (such as CPU utilization and network latency), and sensor output accuracy. Qualitative insights are derived from situational observations during deployment, focusing on the system's adaptability and user-readiness in low-infrastructure environments. This comprehensive design ensures that the MADN is not only technically sound but also practically relevant to the target community.
