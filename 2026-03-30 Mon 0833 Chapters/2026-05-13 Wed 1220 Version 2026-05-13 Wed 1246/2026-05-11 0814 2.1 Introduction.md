Chapter 2 Literature Review

2.1 Introduction

The theoretical and technological foundations of the Modular Adaptive Data Node (MADN) are

rooted in the shift away from centralized computing architectures toward localized, decentralized

models \[24, 26]. This chapter explores the theoretical background of edge computing, distributed networks, precision agriculture, embedded computer vision, and resilient commerce \[24, 26]. In doing so, it situates MADN within a broader effort to design intelligent systems that remain functional, adaptive, and context-aware in environments with limited infrastructure \[34]. It also establishes the conceptual basis for understanding why locally coordinated computing can offer a more resilient alternative to cloud-dependent service delivery in underserved settings \[35]. More specifically, the chapter evaluates the literature through the interconnected lenses of latency, resilience, scalability, affordability, and contextual suitability, since these criteria determine whether advanced computational systems remain practically usable in resource-constrained environments \[26, 34, 35, 37]. It critically reviews existing technologies that attempt to address data-driven challenges in agriculture, public security, and commerce, and explicitly identifies the technological gaps that the MADN framework aims to fill \[25, 27].
