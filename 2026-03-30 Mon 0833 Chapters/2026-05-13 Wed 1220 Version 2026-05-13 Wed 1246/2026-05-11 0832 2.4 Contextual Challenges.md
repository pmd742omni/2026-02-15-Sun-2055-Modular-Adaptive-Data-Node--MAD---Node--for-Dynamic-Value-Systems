2.4 Contextual Challenges

For MADN, the literature is most meaningful when interpreted against the realities of the environ
ments in which the framework is intended to operate. In contexts such as Bulawayo and Tsholotsho,
unreliable connectivity, intermittent power, constrained bandwidth, and the high relative cost of
cloud-reliant services shape not only technical performance but also whether a digital system can
be trusted, sustained, and routinely adopted [14, 30]. These conditions make it insufficient to
evaluate intelligent systems only in terms of algorithmic capability or centralized processing effi
ciency. Instead, suitability depends on whether computation can degrade gracefully, preserve core
functionality during outages, and exploit local resources before resorting to external infrastructure
[33, 34, 35].
4
A further challenge is that resource-constrained environments are not merely low-capacity versions
of well-connected settings; they often require different architectural priorities altogether. Systems
designed primarily for remote processing may optimize for scale and centralized coordination, yet
communities facing unstable connectivity require architectures that prioritize locality, resilience,
recoverability, and selective synchronization [16, 35, 37]. This distinction is important because
it shifts the design question from how to maximize cloud dependence to how to balance local
autonomy with opportunistic access to wider computational resources when conditions allow [31,
36, 37]. It also suggests that success should be measured not only by processing throughput or
model sophistication, but by whether a system remains affordable, maintainable, trustworthy, and
functionally useful under ordinary infrastructural stress [13, 14, 30, 35]
